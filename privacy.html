Lector

Privacy policy

Last updated 12 October 2026

Lector is an ebook reader for Android. It has no servers, no accounts of its own, no advertising and no analytics or crash reporting. The developer does not receive or have access to any of your data.

Data stored on your device

The books you import, your reading positions, highlights, notes, bookmarks, shelves, settings, pronunciation rules and downloaded voices are stored only in Lector's private storage on your device. Uninstalling Lector deletes them.

Google Drive sync (optional)

If you choose to connect a Google account in Settings, Lector asks Google for two permissions:

  • Lector's own app data folder in your Google Drive (drive.appdata). Lector uses it to store copies of your books and your reading data so your devices stay in sync. This is a hidden folder that only Lector can read; Lector cannot see any other files in your Drive.
  • Your email address, used only to show which account is connected.

This data goes directly between your device and Google; it is stored under your Google account and counts toward your Drive storage. You can stop syncing at any time with Disconnect in Settings, delete the data from Google Drive (Settings → Manage apps → Lector → Delete hidden app data), or revoke access at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Lector's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Online services Lector contacts

These requests are made only when you use the related feature, and send only what is needed for it:

  • Google Books and Open Library — a book's title, author or ISBN, to look up covers and details.
  • Merriam-Webster — the word you choose to define. Without a connection, Lector uses its offline dictionary.
  • GitHub — to download read-aloud voices you choose to install, and, every few hours while Lector is open, to check whether a newer version of Lector has been released (a plain request for the release list; nothing about you or your books is sent). Read-aloud itself runs entirely on your device; your text is never sent anywhere to be spoken.

Each of these services handles requests under its own privacy policy and may see your device's IP address, as with any web request.

Children

Lector does not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children.

Changes
